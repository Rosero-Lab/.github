# Swine Applied Innovations Lab

**Iowa State University · Applied research for the future of swine production**

The **Swine Applied Innovations Lab (SAIL)** at Iowa State University develops practical solutions to complex challenges in swine production by combining scientific research with emerging technologies.

Our work connects animal science, nutrition, data, technology, and economics with the goal of producing research and tools that can be applied in real production environments.

## Research Focus

SAIL's work is organized around three primary areas:

### Improving Sow & Pig Livability

We study factors that influence pig and sow health, behavior, welfare, and long-term livability.

Current research includes topics such as welfare-resource access, post-weaning behavior, early feed intake, ingredient preferences, and strategies for supporting animals during vulnerable production stages.

### Advancing Swine Nutrition

Our nutrition research investigates how feed ingredients, nutrient composition, and feeding strategies influence pig performance, health, and productivity.

Areas of study include:

- functional dietary fiber,
- soybean meal inclusion,
- ingredient variability,
- net energy validation,
- amino acid and protein-source variability,
- nursery feed preference,
- and nutritional strategies across production phases.

### Accelerating Smart Farming

SAIL explores how emerging technologies can help producers better understand animals, production systems, and economic outcomes.

Our work includes:

- computer vision,
- artificial intelligence,
- behavioral analysis,
- data analytics,
- digital decision-support tools,
- and technologies that connect biological performance with practical management decisions.

## Applied Tools & Technology

Scientific research is most valuable when it can be translated into useful decisions.

Alongside traditional research, SAIL develops and evaluates digital tools that make research findings easier to apply in real production environments.

### Feed4Profit

**[Feed4Profit](https://feed4profit.net/)** is a web-based decision-support platform developed at Iowa State University to help swine producers and nutritionists evaluate feeding strategies from both biological and economic perspectives.

The platform allows users to design and compare feeding programs, evaluate ingredient and feed costs, model expected pig performance, and compare projected economic outcomes under changing market conditions.

Feed4Profit is designed to connect questions that are often evaluated separately:

**What should we feed? → How might pigs perform? → What could it cost? → What could the economic outcome be?**

Feed4Profit's continued software development and modernization includes collaboration with **[AvidZenith LLC](https://github.com/AvidZenith)** on application architecture, platform development, technical implementation, and the system's long-term software foundation.

Production source code and proprietary implementation details are maintained privately. Public documentation and other appropriate project materials may be published through this organization separately from the production codebase.

## Research, Technology & Industry Application

SAIL operates at the intersection of scientific research and practical application.

That means our work may take many forms:

- controlled research trials,
- data analysis,
- scientific publications,
- presentations,
- producer-focused tools,
- software systems,
- computer-vision research,
- educational resources,
- and collaborative industry projects.

The goal is not simply to develop technology for its own sake, but to determine where technology can meaningfully improve our ability to understand pigs, evaluate production decisions, and translate scientific knowledge into practice.

## About This GitHub Organization

This GitHub organization supports the software and technical side of SAIL's research and applied projects.

Public repositories may contain selected:

- project documentation,
- research tools,
- technical resources,
- educational material,
- examples,
- and open-source software where appropriate.

Many SAIL projects involve active research, unpublished work, proprietary implementation, sensitive infrastructure, or collaborative intellectual property. Those repositories remain private when public release is not appropriate.

A repository being private should therefore not be interpreted as an inactive project.

## Collaboration

SAIL works across disciplines and organizations to move research from scientific questions toward useful outcomes.

Depending on the project, this may include collaboration with researchers, universities, producers, nutritionists, industry organizations, technology developers, and software engineers.

Project-specific collaborators, funding sources, and attribution are documented with the individual research project whenever appropriate.
